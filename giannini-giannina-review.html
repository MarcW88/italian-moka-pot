Giannini Giannina Review

Verdict

A lifetime moka pot for true enthusiasts. The Giannini Giannina combines a patented no-screw lever closure, all-stainless steel construction, Italian manufacturing and a 10-year guarantee. The moka community considers it among the finest stovetop espresso in the world. If you want the best and can stretch your budget, this is a serious contender.

The Giannini Giannina is a classic among stainless steel moka pots. The design dates to 1968, by Carlo Giannini, and has barely changed since: entirely stainless steel, a broad and elegant form and a patented closing system without screw thread. Where most moka pots have a simple screw mechanism, the Giannina uses a sort of "click-lock" with a lever. This makes it different from the well-known aluminium models in both feel and durability.

Material & construction: all stainless, no screws

The Giannina is made entirely from thick stainless steel, placing it immediately in a different category from light aluminium or partially plastic moka pots. The wall is intentionally thicker to conduct heat stably and give a feeling of solidity.

The most remarkable feature is the closure mechanism. Instead of screwing the top onto the bottom, the moka pot clicks into a sort of groove and is locked with a lever. This "no-screw, lock-tight handle snap fastening system", as the manufacturer calls it, ensures a perfect seal and reduces preparation time, as there is no pressure lost through imperfect screwing.

The handle itself is thicker in recent models and designed to stay cool even on gas or electric hobs. Current versions in 1, 3 and 6 cups are induction-compatible. The moka pot is made entirely in Italy and carries a Red Dot Design Award, underscoring the combination of form and function.

Using it: classic moka principle, different closure

In use the Giannina follows the classic moka principle: water in the lower reservoir, ground coffee in the filter funnel, and heat until the coffee rises. The difference lies in how you open and close it. You click the top into the bottom and lock everything with the lever; to open, you release the lever and lift the upper part.

The practical effect is that you never deal with seized threads and the seal is consistent. Multiple users praise the ergonomics: the lever sits well in the hand, opening and closing is smooth, and the moka pot does not leak when correctly locked. The blue silicone gasket is frequently cited explicitly as a strong point: better material than standard rubber gaskets, with a longer lifespan.

Taste & experience among enthusiasts

In the moka community, the Giannina has a reputation as producing "the best stovetop espresso in the world" — a subjective label, but one that says something about the appreciation. Users note that the coffee produced is full and aromatic, with consistent extraction thanks to the airtight closure.

The choice of materials is also appreciated: stainless steel does not react with coffee acids in the way some aluminium pots can over time. This helps maintain a clean taste profile, especially with intensive use. In reviews the pot is often compared to "the well-known market leader" (read: Bialetti), with comments like "the components are truly top quality, better than the well-known market leader" and an overall score of "10/10".

Maintenance & lifespan

Because the Giannina is entirely stainless steel, cleaning is relatively straightforward. The manufacturer recommends removing the blue silicone gasket during intensive vinegar cleaning, and shows in its own instruction videos how to remove deposits and restore the shine by briefly boiling in water-vinegar mixtures and then rinsing thoroughly.

In practice this comes down to:

  • rinse after use with hot water and mild washing-up liquid if needed;
  • occasionally descale and remove deposits, without aggressive abrasives;
  • ensure all parts are completely dry before reassembling, to prevent rust.

The Giannina is often sold with a 10-year guarantee and is known as a "lifetime" purchase, with the caveat that Giannini in Brescia is currently experiencing difficulties and the future of production is uncertain. This makes existing units even more interesting for enthusiasts: those who own one generally treasure it.

Who is the Giannini Giannina for?

The Giannina is clearly aimed at users who:

  • want a durable all-stainless steel moka pot;
  • value ergonomics and the closure mechanism, and are tired of wrestling with seized screw threads;
  • are prepared to invest a little more in a moka pot with a long guarantee and a distinctive design.

For those who primarily want a cheap first moka pot, this is not an entry-level model; the price is higher than standard aluminium models. But for those who want a well-made, robust moka pot that lasts for years and stands clearly apart from the crowd in terms of feel, the Giannina is a very strong candidate.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Giannini Giannina induction compatible?

Yes. The 1, 3 and 6-cup versions are induction-compatible. The fully stainless steel construction with a magnetic base means it works natively on induction hobs without an adapter.

How does the lever closure on the Giannina work?

You place the upper part over the lower part and lock it with the handle lever — a quarter-turn click rather than screwing. To open: release the lever and lift off the top. No cross-threading possible, and the seal is always consistent.

Is the Giannini Giannina still being made?

As of 2025-2026, Giannini in Brescia is facing financial difficulties and production uncertainty. Units are still available from retailers but stock may be limited. If you want one, it is worth acting sooner rather than later.

Giannini Giannina vs Alessi 9090 — which is better?

Both are premium all-stainless design moka pots with induction compatibility and lifetime-level build quality. The 9090 has the stronger design pedigree (MoMA, Compasso d'Oro) and a bayonet closure; the Giannina has the lever lock and blue silicone gasket. Taste-wise both are exceptional — the choice comes down to design preference and closure style.
