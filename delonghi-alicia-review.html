Verdict

A solid choice for those who love strong coffee but do not want to worry about the hob. The Alicia simplifies the moka ritual: fill, press a button, done. Convenient auto shut-off, compact design and consistent taste — ideal for small kitchens, offices or anyone who wants moka-style coffee without the stovetop.

What is the De'Longhi Alicia, exactly?

The De'Longhi Alicia is an electric moka pot: a small coffee appliance that works on the classic Italian moka principle, but from its own base rather than on gas or induction. You fill the bottom with water, the basket with ground coffee, click the carafe onto the base and press a button. The appliance then does everything itself: heat, push the coffee up, switch off and keep warm.

The idea is not to replace an espresso machine, but to simplify the familiar moka ritual. You still get a strong, concentrated coffee — but without having to stand over the hob.

First impression: design and material

Visually, the Alicia is a sort of hybrid between a classic moka pot and a small electric coffee maker. The base is compact and round, the upper carafe has recognisable moka shapes and a transparent lid. The whole thing looks clean and modern, but not luxurious — designed as an ordinary kitchen appliance, not a design object.

The upper chamber is plastic, which you immediately notice if you are used to all-metal moka pots. This makes the carafe lighter and cooler to the touch on the outside, but gives a "plastic" feel in the hand. The base is metallic and snaps fairly solidly onto the electric unit. The appliance is clearly made for everyday use, but feels less indestructible than a simple aluminium moka pot with no electronics.

How to make coffee with it

The process is simple, even for someone who has never touched a moka pot before:

  1. Fill the bottom with water to the mark (half or full capacity);
  2. Fill the filter basket with ground coffee (a medium grind works best), levelled without tamping;
  3. Close the carafe and place it on the base;
  4. Press the button and wait for the appliance to finish.

Instead of monitoring the flame yourself, you let the Alicia run its programme. The appliance switches off automatically once the cycle is complete and keeps the coffee warm for a short time — particularly handy if you cannot pour immediately in the morning but do not want overly strong or burnt coffee.

Taste: what do you get in the cup?

The taste is close to what you expect from a moka pot: strong, fairly concentrated with a pronounced body. It is not espresso from a full machine, but clearly more intense than a standard filter coffee. Those familiar with this style of coffee will immediately recognise the Alicia as "moka-style".

The machine is calibrated for a moderate grind profile. Too fine and you quickly get a sharp, bitter cup with more sediment; too coarse gives a flat, less aromatic taste. Within that window you can very well experiment with different beans and recipes. The big difference from a classic moka pot is that you do not adjust the heat yourself: the cycle runs as it runs. That is reassuring for those who primarily want convenience, but less interesting for those who like to fine-tune.

In daily use

In practice this is typically the kind of appliance you put down, plug in and use in more or less the same way every day. No preheating, no hob to free up, no flame to watch. For small kitchens, offices or student rooms this is a clear advantage: one plug socket is enough, no separate hob required.

Capacity is around six moka cups. In practice that means two to three normal cups if you dilute the coffee slightly or use milk. For large families or extended brunches the volume is rather limited; the Alicia feels more like a "personal appliance" than a central coffee maker.

Maintenance & lifespan

Cleaning is mainly done by hand. The plastic upper carafe and the metal reservoir can simply be rinsed with water and a mild washing-up liquid if needed. The dishwasher is not a good idea, especially not for the plastic parts. The coffee basket should be emptied after each brew; dried grounds quickly make the taste irregular and bitter.

Because there is electronics and a plastic top, you should expect a different "lifespan" from a classic moka pot. A Bialetti in aluminium can still be used after thirty years; the Alicia is realistically an appliance that will need replacing after a number of years. That does not mean it is fragile, but it puts it in a different category from purely mechanical moka pots.

Who is the De'Longhi Alicia a good choice for?

This electric De'Longhi is especially interesting for people who:

  • enjoy strong coffee but do not want to think about the hob;
  • have limited space and want to get by with a single compact appliance;
  • are looking for their first introduction to the moka style but prefer not to use an open flame.

Those who want a minimalist, indestructible moka pot will find better options in a classic aluminium model without a cable. Those who have been happily using a Bialetti on gas for years and enjoy adjusting the flame themselves will see the Alicia more as a supplementary appliance than a replacement.

For everyone who values convenience, auto shut-off and a small keep-warm zone, it is a very logical option. The coffee is strong, the use is simple and the appliance needs little attention — you just choose your own beans and grind.

Frequently asked questions

Does the De'Longhi Alicia need a hob?

No — that is its key advantage. The Alicia has its own electric heating base. You only need a plug socket. This makes it ideal for kitchens without a free hob, offices, student rooms or when travelling.

What grind does the De'Longhi Alicia use?

A medium grind — between filter and espresso — works best. Too fine leads to bitterness and slow flow; too coarse gives a flat, watery taste. Standard pre-ground moka coffee (labelled "moka" or "espresso") usually works well straight out of the bag.

How long does the keep-warm function last?

The Alicia keeps the coffee warm for approximately 30 minutes after the brewing cycle. After that it is recommended to pour or transfer the coffee, as extended warming can make it bitter.

De'Longhi Alicia vs a classic Bialetti — which is better?

They serve different needs. A classic Bialetti is more durable, gives you more control over heat and taste, and lasts decades. The Alicia is simpler, needs no hob and switches off automatically. Choose the Alicia for convenience; choose a Bialetti for control and longevity.
