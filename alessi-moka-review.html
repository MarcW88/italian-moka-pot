Alessi Moka Review

Verdict

An elegant and modern moka pot that surpasses most aluminium models visually, but more prone to bitterness and requiring adjustment. A good choice if you are primarily drawn to the design and willing to experiment; less ideal if you are purely seeking the best taste per pound.

The Alessi Moka — designed by architect David Chipperfield — is clearly a modern, design-led interpretation of the classic Italian moka pot. Its facets, flat lid and reflective surfaces give it a distinctive, almost sculptural appearance. As an object it scores highly: many users are enthusiastic about the look and build quality, clearly more refined than inexpensive small aluminium pots.

In terms of coffee quality, however, reactions are more mixed. Some users praise the Moka for its good coffee and ease of use, but independent tests also flag concerns about bitterness and over-extraction when used "like a normal moka pot".

Design & finish

Visually, the Alessi Moka is a success. It is made from polished aluminium with sharp, graphic lines and a flat lid, immediately distinguishing it from Bialetti's octagonal form. It looks compact, clean and modern, fitting equally well in a minimalist or classic kitchen.

Recurring positives:

  • the whole feels solid and well-finished, with smooth surfaces and precise fit between the parts;
  • the Moka is often explicitly praised for its "iconic" or "elegant" look, especially compared to standard moka pots.

One noted negative is the plastic handle: it looks fine, but some testers find it feels slightly cheap relative to the price point.

Coffee taste: strong, but quickly bitter if you are not careful

In taste tests, the Alessi Moka stands out — but not always positively. Detailed reviews report consistently very bitter, over-extracted coffee even with careful grind and technique adjustment. The suggested cause is an unfavourable ratio between the water reservoir and the coffee basket, meaning standard filling produces too much coffee for too little water.

This means you almost always need to deviate from the "classic moka ritual" to achieve a more pleasant cup. Using less coffee can reduce bitterness, but it requires experimentation.

Important nuance:

  • those who enjoy very strong coffee or drink it primarily with milk sometimes find the results perfectly good — the coffee works well in milk-based drinks;
  • other reviews conversely say "great coffee and easy to use", suggesting experience varies considerably based on taste and usage style.

In short: the Alessi Moka can produce good, strong coffee, but the margin between "pleasantly strong" and "too bitter" is narrower than for better-calibrated moka pots.

Ease of use & maintenance

In terms of use, the Alessi Moka is a fairly typical moka pot: fill the bottom with water, the basket with ground coffee, screw together and place on the hob. The lid lever is functional and the parts are easy to disassemble.

Some strengths:

  • few parts, easy to rinse and clean;
  • no complex mechanics — you master it quickly if you have used a moka pot before;
  • the moka pot dries relatively fast after washing, noted positively by some users.

Some ergonomic negatives:

  • there is no clear water level indicator inside the bottom — you always fill to just below the safety valve;
  • the lid lever is slightly less comfortable than a classic central knob on a Bialetti, making opening less intuitive;
  • the plastic handle can feel cheap relative to the overall design.

For maintenance, this is a fairly straightforward moka pot: regular rinsing, removing coffee residues and occasional gasket replacement. Gaskets are available directly from Alessi, though one review notes that model and part naming can be confusing.

Durability & price positioning

Like most quality moka pots, the Alessi Moka is inherently durable: the aluminium is reasonably solid and with replaceable gaskets you can extend its lifespan easily. Compared to stainless steel models (like the Alessi 9090), aluminium is less impact-resistant, but more than sufficient for normal daily use.

Pricewise, the Moka sits above a standard Bialetti Moka Express but below stainless steel models like the 9090 or La Conica. You are clearly paying a design premium, but not the "museum-piece" prices of other Alessi icons. For some that is the sweet spot; for others the price feels high relative to coffee quality.

Who is the Alessi Moka a good choice for?

The Alessi Moka is most interesting if:

  • you are drawn primarily to the design: you want a modern, graphic moka pot that looks better than average;
  • you like experimenting with dosing and taste and do not mind deviating from standard moka rules (e.g. using slightly less coffee) to find a better balance;
  • you want a compact, visually strong moka pot that doubles as a kitchen object.

It is less suitable if:

  • you want the absolute best moka taste without adjustment — a Bialetti Moka Express or Alessi 9090 perform more consistently;
  • you are primarily price-conscious and want a simple, forgiving beginner moka pot;
  • you have low tolerance for bitterness or over-extraction and do not want to adapt your routine.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Alessi Moka induction compatible?

No. The Alessi Moka is made from aluminium and is not induction-compatible. For induction use, look at stainless steel Alessi models such as the 9090, or use an induction adapter disc.

Why does the Alessi Moka taste bitter?

The most common cause is the water-to-coffee ratio: many users report the default fill level produces an over-extracted, bitter cup. Try using slightly less coffee in the basket, keep the heat low, and remove from the hob immediately when you hear the first gurgling. This typically improves the cup considerably.

Alessi Moka vs Bialetti Moka Express — which is better?

The Bialetti Moka Express is cheaper, more forgiving, produces consistently good moka and has decades of refinement behind it. The Alessi Moka looks more distinctive and modern but requires more technique. Unless design is a priority, the Moka Express is the more rational choice.

Where can I find replacement gaskets for the Alessi Moka?

Replacement gaskets are available directly from Alessi and from specialist kitchenware retailers. Note that model naming in the Alessi parts catalogue can be confusing — it helps to have the exact model code from the box or Alessi's website when ordering.
