The Bialetti Mini Express: a moka pot that brews directly into two cups

The Bialetti Mini Express is a moka pot that pours directly into two espresso cups. Instead of an upper reservoir, it has a small platform with two spouts; you place your cups on it and the coffee flows directly into the cups while the moka pot is on the hob. It is a fun design that combines the classic Bialetti experience with a hint of "coffee bar" atmosphere at home, but it requires slightly more careful use than a regular Moka Express.

Design & material

The Mini Express consists of an aluminium base with water reservoir and filter — like a standard moka pot — and an upper section with two spouts and a small cup platform. The most common retail version is designed for 2 espresso cups per brew, and is often sold without cups; Bialetti also sells sets with matching glass cups.

Key facts to know:

  • the moka pot is aluminium — not induction-compatible without an adapter;
  • it is designed for exactly two cups, although you can direct the flow into a single cup by adjusting the cup placement;
  • the compact format makes it very light and handy for travel, but also slightly more sensitive to positioning and heat distribution.

Users often describe the look as "beautiful but demanding": the design draws attention, but not everyone finds it practical for daily use.

How it works: moka principle, but directly into the cups

Internally the Mini Express works like a classic moka pot:

  • water in the lower reservoir, just below the safety valve;
  • fill the filter basket with espresso grind (fine to medium), lightly levelled;
  • screw on the upper section with spouts;
  • place on low to medium heat, with the cups on the platform.

After a few minutes the coffee starts flowing directly from both spouts into the cups. It is spectacular to watch — you can see the whole process — but it also means the cups are very close to the heat source. Testers and users emphasise that the cups get very hot and you need to wait a moment before picking them up.

Flavour profile: strong, concentrated coffee in true moka style — between filter coffee and espresso, without the 9 bars of an espresso machine, but full-bodied and aromatic.

In practice: strengths and pitfalls

Strengths:

  • the ritual is enjoyable — you literally watch the coffee appear in your cups;
  • the quantity is perfect for two small espressos;
  • cleaning is simple: let it cool, unscrew, remove the grounds, rinse.

Often mentioned pitfalls:

  • if the heat is too high, the Mini Express can splutter and you end up with coffee everywhere;
  • some users report that over time the spouts no longer align perfectly with the platform, causing the stream to miss the centre of the cups;
  • there is a real risk of uneven flow from the two spouts, especially if the hob is not perfectly level or the moka pot is not well centred.

As one review neatly summarises: "Nice design, good quantity for two cups. Shame that when the coffee comes out, water splashes the saucers."

Tips for better results

From video reviews and community experiences, a few practical tips emerge:

  • Use low heat: as soon as you hear the first gurgling and the cups start filling, turn off the heat to prevent spluttering and burnt flavours;
  • Level surface: place the moka pot on a perfectly flat hob and make sure the cups are well centred under the spouts to limit uneven flow;
  • Wait before picking up: the cups become very hot, especially if using thin glass or porcelain — give them a minute to cool slightly.

If you follow these tips, the Mini Express remains a pleasurable and functional moka pot for two. If you ignore them, you will quickly discover the "beautiful but demanding" side.

Who is the Bialetti Mini Express for?

The Mini Express is best suited to people who:

  • enjoy espresso-style coffee as a couple and appreciate watching the brewing process;
  • are already familiar with moka-style coffee and are comfortable working at low heat;
  • are looking for a fun, occasional-use moka pot rather than a primary appliance for the whole household.

Those primarily seeking stability and simplicity are often better served by a standard Moka Express: less spectacle, but more predictability. Those who appreciate the charm of two cups filling live and are willing to play a little with heat and positioning will find the Bialetti Mini Express a remarkable and characterful moka pot.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the Bialetti Mini Express splutter?

Spluttering is almost always caused by too high a heat. Use low heat and turn off the hob as soon as the coffee starts flowing. This also prevents over-extraction and burnt flavours.

Can you use the Mini Express for one cup?

Yes, by holding a single cup under both spouts, or by directing both spouts over one larger cup. The pot is designed for two, but adapting is possible with a little creativity.

Is the Mini Express induction compatible?

No, not without an induction adapter disc. The aluminium body is not magnetic and therefore not detected by induction hobs. With an adapter it will work, though the uneven heat distribution of an adapter can affect flow consistency.

Does the Mini Express come with cups?

It depends on the set you buy. Many retailers sell the Mini Express alone; Bialetti also offers bundles with two matching glass espresso cups. Check the product listing carefully before ordering if you want the cups included.
