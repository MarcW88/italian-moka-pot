How do we compare moka pots?

Our comparisons are not based on spec sheets or quick first impressions. Every model we compare is used in practice for at least 3 months and tested directly alongside the alternatives.

Phase 1: First impression

  • Unboxing and checking build quality
  • Evaluating comfort, balance and handle
  • 1–2 weeks of daily use

Phase 2: Intensive testing

  • Different grind and bean combinations
  • Comparison across different hob types
  • Testing maintenance frequency and cleaning

Phase 3: Long term

  • Observing wear after 3+ months
  • Stability of taste and performance
  • Checking availability of spare parts

Notes: we rate each model on coffee taste (30%), build quality (20%), ease of use (15%), maintenance (15%), value for money (10%) and durability (10%). Where personal taste preference comes into play, we mention this explicitly.

Bialetti vs Alessi: which brand suits you best?

Bialetti invented the moka pot (since 1933) and focuses on tradition, ease of use and affordability. Alessi is the design innovator with award-winning moka pots like the Pulcina — more expensive, but with a bold style and premium finish.

Choose Bialetti if…

  • Budget and value for money matter (± £20–45).
  • You appreciate the classic Italian look (Moka, Fiammetta).
  • You want a proven brand with easily available spare parts.

Choose Alessi if…

  • Design and aesthetics are your absolute priority (± £70–120).
  • You want a moka pot that also serves as a design object in the kitchen.
  • You are willing to pay more for the finish and brand status.

Aluminium vs Stainless Steel

The most fundamental choice: lighter and cheaper aluminium, or more durable and induction-compatible stainless steel. In our tests coffee taste is practically identical; the real difference is in hob compatibility, maintenance and aesthetics.

Budget

Choose based on what you realistically want to spend and what you get for it.

£20–40: Bialetti Moka Express or Fiammetta — perfect coffee without frills.

£40–70: Bialetti Venus or Brikka — stainless or crema function, superior finish.

£70+: Alessi Pulcina — you pay for the design and brand experience.

Household size

The right size determines whether your moka pot is practical or frustrating to use.

1–2 people: 3-cup model (e.g. Fiammetta) — ideal for daily use.

3–4 people: 6-cup model (e.g. Venus or Musa) — to serve family and guests in one go.

5+ people / office: 9–12-cup model, or two 6-cup models for more flexibility.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between Bialetti and Alessi?

Bialetti focuses on tradition, ease of use and affordability; Alessi on design and premium finish. In blind tests scores are close, but Bialetti generally wins on value for money, while Alessi stands out on aesthetics and build quality. For most people Bialetti is the logical choice; Alessi is ideal if your moka pot must also be a design object.

Does an expensive moka pot really make better coffee than a cheap one?

Not necessarily. A Bialetti Moka Express at £25 can produce practically the same coffee flavour as an Alessi Pulcina at £90. Pricier models score mainly on finish, design and sometimes durability. The biggest taste differences come from the coffee beans, grind and brewing method — not the price of the moka pot.

How do I choose between aluminium and stainless steel?

If you have induction, stainless steel is essential. On gas or electric, aluminium is often the best value choice: lighter, cheaper and classic. On taste, the difference is minimal. Stainless steel wins if you want less maintenance, a modern look or maximum flexibility across hob types.

Which moka pot size is the most versatile?

In practice, a 6-cup moka pot is the most versatile: large enough for a family or guests, but still usable for two people if you do not fill it completely. 3-cup is ideal for one person or a couple, 9+ cups mainly for large families or the office.

Which comparison should I read first if I am completely stuck?

Start with the comparison that resolves your biggest doubt: material (aluminium vs stainless), hob (induction vs gas) or brand (Bialetti vs Alessi). If you do not yet know which type or budget suits you, start with the buying guide and then come back to the specific comparisons.

Does a stainless steel moka pot also work on gas?

Yes. A stainless steel moka pot works on all hobs: induction, gas, electric and ceramic glass, as long as the base is flat. On gas you have no advantage over aluminium, but you retain the flexibility to switch to induction later without buying a new moka pot.

Which moka pot makes the best coffee?

The taste difference between good moka pots is smaller than many people think. A solid basic model around £25–40 can make practically the same coffee as a much more expensive design model. Exception: the Bialetti Brikka, which thanks to the special valve produces a fuller, espresso-type coffee with crema.
