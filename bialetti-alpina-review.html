Bialetti Alpina Review

Verdict

The Bialetti Alpina is a classic Moka Express with a fun alpine design. The coffee is the trusted Bialetti classic, but the unique look makes it a conversation piece. Ideal as a gift or as a second moka pot for those who enjoy original kitchen objects. Not induction-compatible, but perfect on gas and electric.

The Bialetti Moka Alpina is essentially a classic Moka Express dressed up for the mountains: lacquered bright green, with a lid shaped like an alpine hat and a small spring at the top. You are not buying a different brewing method here, but the same proven moka principle in a packaging that immediately sets itself down on the worktop and invites a smile. It is a model that makes you grin while brewing genuinely serious coffee.

Design: Moka Express with an alpine wink

In terms of base shape, the Alpina is 100% recognisable as a Bialetti: octagonal aluminium body, classic silhouette, three parts and a side safety valve. The difference: the body is lacquered green, the handle is black and the lid is shaped as a small alpine hat. The spring is a plastic detail that seems sturdy enough, but should be kept well away from high flames. In practice it is a compact 3-cup moka pot (around 120–150 ml of coffee), ideal for one or two coffee drinkers. It is not a large family moka pot, but a small, characterful pot for daily moka or as a special "second" moka pot.

Coffee: the trusted Bialetti taste

Since the internal structure is identical to a Moka Express, the Alpina behaves exactly the same in the cup. Fill the bottom to just below the valve, use a fine to medium-fine grind, fill the filter basket without tamping and place on low to medium heat. A few minutes later you have a rich, aromatic moka coffee exactly as expected from Bialetti: full-bodied, slightly syrupy and strong enough to drink straight or use as a base for cappuccino. The Alpina adds no crema gadget or pressure mechanism like the Brikka; the added value is entirely in the look, not in a different taste profile.

In use: small, simple and enjoyable

In daily use this is a very forgiving moka pot. Anyone who has already used a Moka Express has nothing new to learn. Thanks to the compact 3-cup format, the Alpina heats quickly and brews coffee within minutes — making it an ideal personal moka pot: one large moka or two small cups is what you can expect from it. The heat-resistant plastic handle stays manageable with correct use; just make sure the flame stays under the base and does not lick up against the hat and spring.

Being a special or limited design, the Alpina also makes a perfect gift. You get all the simplicity of a Moka Express but in a package that turns heads at brunch or après-ski.

Maintenance & lifespan

Maintenance is identical to any aluminium Bialetti: not the dishwasher, just a simple rinse with hot water after use, with a little mild washing-up liquid if needed. The lacquer is quality, but stays prettier if you avoid aggressive products and abrasive sponges. Leave the moka pot open to dry after cleaning, so the gasket does not remain needlessly damp. The rubber gasket and metal filter are replaceable; by renewing them occasionally you can use the same Alpina for many years.

The only notably more fragile area compared to a standard Moka Express is the plastic spring on the lid. It is sturdy enough for normal use, but avoid dropping the moka pot or consistently using it on very high flames. With that in mind, the Alpina has a perfectly comparable lifespan to a regular Bialetti.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Bialetti Alpina suitable for daily use?

Yes. In our test the Alpina performed like a normal Bialetti moka: sturdy enough for daily use as long as you treat the aluminium and lacquer with care. It is far more than just a collector's piece.

Does it taste like the Moka Express?

Very much so. In practice we tasted a typical Bialetti moka coffee with plenty of body. The main difference between the two models lies in the design and appearance, not really in the taste.

Is the Bialetti Alpina worth the extra cost?

For purely functional use, a Moka Express is often rationally sufficient. In our experience, the Alpina is especially worth the extra if you care about design and the story behind it — for example as a gift or collector's piece.

Is the Alpina good as a first moka pot?

Absolutely. The learning curve in our test was identical to other Bialetti models. If you want something special from the outset rather than just "the standard", this is a great way to start with moka.
